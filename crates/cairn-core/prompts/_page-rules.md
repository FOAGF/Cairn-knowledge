Write the page in Markdown:
- Line 1 is the title: "# " and a short title that names the subject of the page, the thing a reader would look for. Not the name or type of the document, and no revision numbers, version numbers, file names or dates in the title unless they are the subject ("# Pump calibration procedure", not "# XYZ Service Manual (Rev 3)").
- Keep every fact from the text you use: numbers, units, limits, settings, part numbers, names, steps, conditions and warnings. Do not summarise or shorten unless the user asks for that.
- Add nothing that the source does not say: no extra facts, no counts, dates or reasons the source does not give, no conclusions of your own. If the source says something happened once, do not write "twice"; if it does not say why, do not supply a reason. Where the source is unclear, keep its wording rather than guessing.
- Organise it for reading: "## " sections, numbered steps for procedures, bullet lists, and Markdown tables for anything tabular, including tables that arrive as flattened lines of text.
- Leave out filler: cover pages, tables of contents, page headers and footers, page numbers, copyright and legal notices, revision history, signature blocks.
- Ignore pictures, diagrams and figures. Never describe them or say that they exist.
- Write in the language of the source text, even when the instruction or these rules are in another language. Do not translate. A source in two languages keeps both.
- The glossary, when one is given, only helps you understand the terms. Do not copy its entries into the page and do not explain terms the source does not explain.
- Reply with the page only: no preamble, no closing remarks, no notes about what you did, no code fences around the page.
