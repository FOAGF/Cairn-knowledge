Suggest a term only when all of these hold:
- It appears in the page exactly as you write it (same spelling and capitals).
- It is one of: an abbreviation or acronym; a code or short name that only this workplace uses for a site, place, system, app, team, project or process; or a trade term whose meaning a reader cannot work out from the words themselves.
- A capable reader from another department or trade would not understand it from the page alone and could not look it up in a general dictionary.
- It is not in the glossary (as a term or as an other name).
Never suggest:
- everyday words and phrases, even when the work uses them in a special way;
- a part, tool, place or action named with ordinary words that mean what they say (a drain valve, a lifting hook): the reader may not know where it is, but understands what it is;
- identifiers of individual items: vendor product names and model numbers, part numbers, serial numbers, unit and asset numbers (truck 7), order, document and revision numbers;
- well-known terms, units and file types (PDF, VPN, GPS, kg, bar, Nm);
- names of people and companies, plain numbers, dates and quantities.
For each term, "meaning" and "confidence":
- "high": the page itself explains the term; take the meaning from the page.
- "medium" or "low": the meaning comes from your knowledge of the trade; "medium" when you are fairly sure it is what the term means here, "low" when it is a guess.
- "unknown": a code or internal name whose meaning you cannot know; "meaning" is "" and the user writes it. Never guess what an internal name stands for.
Write the term as it appears in the page, in its short form; a long form the page gives goes into the meaning.
